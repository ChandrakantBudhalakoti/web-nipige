If you are researching how to build a service marketplace, you want to connect people who need a service, home cleaning, repairs, tutoring, beauty, with the providers who deliver it, all under your brand. This guide covers what you have to build and the three ways to build it.

A service marketplace is not one app. It is a few connected pieces, and the parts you do not see as a customer are where the real work sits.

## What is a service marketplace?

A service marketplace is a branded platform where **customers find, book, and pay service providers in one place**, while you, the owner, run the marketplace and set the commission or subscription. Thumbtack, Urban Company, and TaskRabbit are service marketplaces. So is a branded home-services or professional-services platform you launch yourself.

## Why build your own service marketplace?

Demand is large and still climbing. The global gig-economy tech-platforms market is set to grow from about **$485 billion in 2025 to $1.39 trillion by 2035**, a CAGR of 11.1%, according to [Fact.MR](https://www.factmr.com/report/gig-economy-tech-platforms-market). Roughly **1.57 billion people freelance worldwide**, close to 46% of the global workforce, per [Demandsage](https://www.demandsage.com/gig-economy-statistics/).

Providers already work this way. The gap is a trusted place to find and book them. Owning that platform means you set the terms, you keep the customer relationship, and your providers stop paying per-lead fees to a platform they do not control.

## What are the parts of a service marketplace?

Every service marketplace needs these pieces working together, all under your brand:

- **A customer app.** Browse and search providers, view profiles and ratings, book or request a service, track the job, and pay in-app.
- **A provider panel.** Each provider manages their services and availability, accepts or declines jobs, handles bookings, and gets paid, with their data kept separate.
- **An admin console.** You approve providers, verify them (KYC), set categories, commission, and zones, handle disputes, and see the whole marketplace.
- **A payments layer.** It takes the customer's payment and splits it between you and the provider automatically, through a processor like Stripe or PayPal.

The customer app is the part people see. The provider panel, the verification, and the admin controls are the majority of the build, and they are what a cheap quote tends to leave out.

## How to build a service marketplace, step by step

1. **Pick one service category and one city.** Focused marketplaces reach liquidity faster. Start with one trade, such as cleaning or repairs, in one area.
2. **Sign your first providers.** Onboard 10 to 20 providers who will actually take bookings on day one. Supply first, always.
3. **Design the money flow.** Decide your commission or flat subscription, who pays any service fee, and when providers get paid.
4. **Choose your build route** (custom, generic builder, or no-code platform, compared below).
5. **Set up provider verification and trust.** Provider sign-up, KYC, reviews, and clear policies. This is the step new marketplaces underestimate most.
6. **Connect payments and launch in one area.** Go live with your committed providers and a service area you can cover well.
7. **Grow providers and demand together.** Add the services your customers ask for, and market to the customers your providers bring.

## The three ways to build one

### 1. Custom build with a development company

You get exactly what you spec, and a bill and timeline to match: typically **$30,000 to $300,000+ over 4 to 9 months**. You own the code, and every server, update, and change afterward. Right only when your marketplace needs something no platform offers.

### 2. A generic app builder

Tools like Bubble or a marketplace script let you assemble something without a full dev team, but you still spend weeks wiring up bookings, provider profiles, verification, and payments that were not purpose-built for a services marketplace. You trade money for your own time.
